Why do old photos and videos look blurry on my iPhone?

Updated September 30, 2026

Short answer

Usually because Optimize iPhone Storage is on. It’s the default when you use iCloud Photos: the full-resolution original is stored in iCloud and your iPhone keeps a smaller, space-saving version. When you open an older photo or video you see that smaller version until the original downloads, and offline or on a weak connection, it may not download at all.

Nothing has been lost; the original is still in iCloud. To see it, give it a moment on a good connection. To stop it happening, turn on Download and Keep Originals, or keep copies of the few that matter outside the Photos library.

What Optimize iPhone Storage actually does

Apple describes it this way: when Optimize iPhone Storage is on, “all your full‑resolution photos and videos are stored in iCloud in their original formats, with storage-saving versions kept on your iPhone.” It’s on by default when you turn on iCloud Photos.

The swap isn’t something you trigger, and Apple doesn’t document which items go first. Your iPhone replaces local originals with smaller versions when it needs space. In practice, recent photos usually stay full size, which is why it tends to be older photos and videos that look soft.

Videos show it most. A 4K clip replaced by a small version looks obviously soft on a large screen, and long videos take the longest to download back.

How to tell if that’s what you’re seeing

  • It sharpens after a few seconds. Open the photo, wait on Wi-Fi. If it snaps into focus, the original just downloaded. Photos usually shows a small progress indicator while it does.
  • It stays soft offline. In Airplane Mode, older items stay blurry and recent ones look fine.
  • Check the setting. Open Settings › [your name] › iCloud › Photos. In iOS 27, tap iPhone Storage to see which option is selected.

Other reasons a photo or video looks blurry

If the setting is off, or the item never sharpens, it’s probably one of these. In each case the full-resolution version may never have existed on your iPhone.

How to fix it

See the original now

Connect to Wi-Fi, open the photo or video and wait for it to download. Large videos can take a while.

Keep every original on your iPhone

Go to Settings › [your name] › iCloud › Photos (in iOS 27, then iPhone Storage) and choose Download and Keep Originals. Your iPhone then needs room for your whole library. Check Settings › General › iPhone Storage first. Compare the two settings.

Keep only the ones that matter

There’s no switch to keep one particular photo on the device while the rest stay optimized. The workaround is to keep a copy outside the Photos library: export the unmodified original to the Files app or a drive, or use an app built for it. Here are the options.
