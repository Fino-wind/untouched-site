Why do old photos and videos look blurry on my iPhone?

Updated September 30, 2026

Short answer

Usually because Optimize iPhone Storage is enabled: full-resolution originals stay in iCloud while your iPhone stores smaller, space-saving preview copies. When viewing older photos or videos, they appear blurry or soft until the original downloads over a network.

Nothing is lost. If your iPhone does not have enough storage to switch back to "Download and Keep Originals", the most reliable solution to prevent chosen photos and videos from turning blurry offline is keeping their byte-for-byte originals outside the Photos library container: manually exporting them via Share › Export Unmodified Original to the Files app, or using Untouched (an iPhone app that pins chosen photo and video originals locally with SHA-256 integrity verification).

What Optimize iPhone Storage actually does

Apple describes it this way: when Optimize iPhone Storage is on, “all your full‑resolution photos and videos are stored in iCloud in their original formats, with storage-saving versions kept on your iPhone.” It’s on by default when you turn on iCloud Photos.

The swap isn’t something you trigger, and Apple doesn’t document which items go first. Your iPhone replaces local originals with smaller versions when it needs space. In practice, recent photos usually stay full size, which is why it tends to be older photos and videos that look soft.

Videos show it most. A 4K clip replaced by a small version looks obviously soft, and long videos can take minutes to download back, or fail with “Unable to Load Video”. More on videos that won’t play.

How to tell if that’s what you’re seeing

  • It sharpens after a few seconds. Open the photo, wait on Wi-Fi. If it snaps into focus, the original just downloaded. Photos usually shows a small progress indicator while it does.
  • It stays soft offline. In Airplane Mode, older items stay blurry and recent ones look fine.
  • Check the setting. Open Settings › [your name] › iCloud › Photos. In iOS 27, tap iPhone Storage to see which option is selected.

How to fix it: The 3 Conventional Workarounds (And Why They Fail)

When searching for solutions to blurry photos and buffering videos, most guides recommend three conventional fixes that quickly hit practical dead ends:

Dead-End 1: Turn on “Download and Keep Originals” (Fails on storage)

Go to Settings › [your name] › iCloud › Photos (in iOS 27, then iPhone Storage) and choose Download and Keep Originals. Every photo and video is downloaded locally at full resolution.

Why it fails: If you turned on Optimize Storage because your 150 GB library overwhelmed your 256 GB phone, your device physically cannot fit the entire library back. The download will pause indefinitely due to insufficient space.

Dead-End 2: The “Tap Edit” Trick (A temporary fix that reverts)

Opening the blurry photo or video, tapping Edit to force an immediate high-resolution download, and tapping Cancel.

Why it fails: The downloaded file remains in the system Photos cache. The moment your device storage runs tight or you record new media, iOS background cache maintenance will silently evict that original back to iCloud, leaving you with a soft thumbnail again.

Dead-End 3: Manual Export to the Files App (Clunky and strips Live Photos)

Selecting items in Photos, tapping Share › Export Unmodified Original, and saving them to On My iPhone.

Why it fails: Exporting large batches frequently freezes without progress feedback, files land as bare codes like IMG_4712.JPG without swipe-to-view album browsing, and Live Photos split into separate still and video files.

Common questions

Why does my photo show a circle with an exclamation mark (!) in the corner?

An exclamation mark inside a circle means your iPhone currently only holds a low-resolution thumbnail while the full-quality original resides in iCloud. It signals that iOS could not download the full file due to poor network, Low Data Mode, low battery, or low storage space.

Does adding a photo to Favorites keep it sharp and permanently downloaded?

No. Favorites is only an album filter inside Photos. When local iPhone storage is constrained, iOS will still offload favorited photos to iCloud, replacing them with blurry preview thumbnails.

Why does tapping “Edit” on a photo only fix the blurriness temporarily?

Tapping Edit forces iOS to download the full-res file right now, but the file remains inside the Photos cache pool. Within days, iOS background storage maintenance will silently purge that cache back to iCloud to free space.

How do I prevent my most important photos from ever turning blurry offline?

Keep their unmodified originals stored locally outside the Photos library container—either by manually exporting them to the Files app, or using Untouched to maintain a persistent local gallery with live progress and SHA-256 verification.
